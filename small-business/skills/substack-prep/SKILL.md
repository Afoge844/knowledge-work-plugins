# Substack Prep — Anchor-to-Asset Engine

One newsletter essay → 7 derivative assets → full week of content across all platforms. This skill does the entire transformation in one pass.

## Input

Accept the essay in one of three ways:
1. Pasted directly into the conversation
2. Notion page URL — fetch and extract the content
3. Google Doc URL — fetch and extract

If none provided, ask: "Paste your newsletter essay or share the Notion link and I'll transform it."

---

## Step 1 — Analyze the Essay

Before generating assets, extract:
- **Core thesis:** The one sentence the whole essay is built around
- **Key story:** The personal narrative or experience anchor
- **Transformation arc:** The before → after the reader experiences
- **Top 3 insights:** The most quotable, standalone ideas
- **Target reader moment:** The specific situation in the reader's life this essay speaks to
- **Emotional tone:** What feeling does this essay land on?

Present this analysis briefly: "Here's what I'm working with before I build the assets." Wait for a quick thumbs-up or correction before proceeding — this 30-second checkpoint prevents building 7 assets from a wrong reading.

---

## Step 2 — Build All 7 Assets

Build all assets in one pass. Label each clearly.

---

### ASSET 1: Substack Newsletter — Final Polish Pass

Review the essay for:
- Does the opening hook land in the first 2 sentences?
- Is there a clear "I was you" bridge moment?
- Does the CTA at the end tell the reader exactly what to do?
- Is the tone mentor, not guru — conversational, not corporate?

Output: brief editorial notes only (do not rewrite the full essay unless Art asks). Flag 1–3 specific sentences to strengthen.

---

### ASSET 2: 21 Substack Notes

Generate 21 Notes derived from the essay. Each Note is:
- 1–4 sentences max
- Standalone — makes complete sense without reading the essay
- A single insight, observation, question, or quote
- Ends with curiosity or resonance, not a hard sell
- Voice: mentor, direct, grounded

Organize into 3 weeks of 7 Notes each. Label: "Week 1 Notes", "Week 2 Notes", "Week 3 Notes."

Each week should have a mix of:
- 2 insight Notes (a key idea from the essay)
- 2 story Notes (a moment or scene from the essay)
- 1 question Note (a provocative question the essay raises)
- 1 quote Note (a pull quote from the essay, attributed to Art)
- 1 CTA Note (soft — invites to read the full essay, join Skool, or reply)

---

### ASSET 3: LinkedIn Long-Form Post

Structure:
- **Hook line** (1 sentence — must stop the scroll)
- **3-line opening** (the tension or problem — short punchy lines, one per line)
- **Bridge paragraph** (the "I was you" moment — personal and specific)
- **Core insight** (the key lesson, told through story, not bullet points)
- **Takeaway** (1–2 sentences — what the reader should do or think differently about)
- **CTA** (soft — "If this resonates, here's where to go deeper:" + Substack or Skool link)

Tone: Same as the essay. No hashtag dumps. Max 2 hashtags if any.

---

### ASSET 4: LinkedIn Carousel Outline

Structure for a 7–10 slide carousel:
- Slide 1: Hook — bold statement or question (matches the essay's core tension)
- Slide 2: The problem (who this is for and what they're facing)
- Slide 3–6: 4 key insights or steps from the essay (one per slide — headline + 1 sentence)
- Slide 7–8: The transformation (before → after)
- Slide 9: Quote slide (most powerful line from the essay)
- Slide 10: CTA (Substack or Skool)

Output: slide-by-slide outline with headline and body text for each slide. Art takes this into Gamma AI to build the visual.

---

### ASSET 5: Email Hook (for Rainmaker/GHL broadcast)

A 4-paragraph email to Art's list:
- **Subject line:** 3 options (curiosity-driven, benefit-driven, question-based)
- **Para 1:** The hook — the reader's pain or situation, named specifically
- **Para 2:** The story bridge — Art's "I was you" moment from the essay
- **Para 3:** The insight tease — what the essay teaches without giving it all away
- **Para 4:** CTA — read the full essay on Substack + one-line Skool mention

Tone: Warm. Like writing to a friend. Not a marketing email.

---

### ASSET 6: Podcast Script (solo episode)

Structure for a 10–15 minute solo episode:
- **Intro** (30 sec): "This week I want to talk about [topic]. If you've ever [relatable situation], stay with me."
- **Context-setting** (2–3 min): Why this topic matters right now for encore entrepreneurs
- **The story** (3–4 min): The personal experience from the essay — told in full narrative mode
- **The insight** (3–4 min): What the story taught, broken into 2–3 clear points
- **Application** (2 min): What the listener can do with this insight today
- **Outro** (1 min): CTA to Substack + Skool + "see you next week"

Output: full script, written for speaking aloud — conversational, not written-English formal.

---

### ASSET 7: Skool Community Prompt

A discussion prompt to post in the TEA Skool community based on the essay's core question or insight.

Structure:
- 1 sentence framing the topic
- The key question from the essay, made personal: "When did you first [experience this]?"
- An invitation: "Share below — I'll reply to every one this week."

Tone: Same warmth as Art's Substack but slightly more conversational — this is community, not broadcast.

---

### BONUS: X/Twitter Thread (if Art uses X)

8–12 tweets:
- Tweet 1: Hook (the essay's core tension as a bold statement)
- Tweets 2–9: One insight per tweet — sequential, builds to a conclusion
- Tweet 10: The takeaway
- Tweet 11: CTA (Substack link + "Full essay here:")
- Tweet 12 (optional): Quote from the essay — standalone, retweetable

---

## Step 3 — Voice Check Reminder

After presenting all 7 assets:

> "These are ready for your voice checkpoint. Run them through oJoy/Project Shepard before anything goes live. That's the quality gate — not optional."

Then offer:
- "Want me to save all of these to a Notion page or Google Doc?"
- "Want me to put the 21 Notes into a scheduling-ready format?"

---

## Guardrails

- **Write in Art's voice throughout.** Mentor, not guru. Conversational, warm, direct. No hype language. No "unlock your potential" phrases.
- **Never publish directly.** These are drafts for voice checkpoint review.
- **The essay is the source of truth.** Do not introduce ideas not present in the original essay.
- **"I was you" framing is mandatory** in the LinkedIn post and email. It's Art's signature bridge.
